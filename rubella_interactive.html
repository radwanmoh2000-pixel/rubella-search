Introduction: What is Rubella?

Rubella (German Measles) is a contagious respiratory infection. It typically presents as a mild disease in adults, characterized by a maculopapular rash.

  • Transmission: Airborne respiratory droplets.
  • Incubation: 12 to 23 days.

Mild for the mother, devastating for the fetus.

Morphology & Structure

  • Shape: Spherical to pleomorphic (50–70 nm).
  • Genome: Single-stranded, positive-sense RNA [(+)ssRNA].
  • Envelope: Contains viral spikes (E1 and E2 glycoproteins).

Effect on Pregnancy: Fetal Risk Zone

90% Risk of Anomalies

If maternal infection occurs during the first 10-12 weeks of gestation, the virus crosses the placenta unhindered.

Pathogenesis & Spread

Maternal Infection

  • Virus enters respiratory tract and replicates in local lymph nodes.
  • Viremia occurs, spreading to skin and organs.

Fetal Infection

  • Virus infects the placenta (Placentitis).
  • Enters fetal circulation, causing apoptosis and stopping cell mitosis.

Congenital Rubella Syndrome (CRS)

When the virus infects the fetus, it results in the severe Classic Teratogenic Triad.

Let's view these three conditions ⬇

1. Ocular Defects (Cataract)

Development of Cataracts (clouding of the lens) and Microphthalmia.

2. Auditory Defects (Deafness)

Sensorineural Hearing Loss is the most common standalone defect, affecting 60% of infants.

3. Cardiac Defects

Mainly causes Patent Ductus Arteriosus (PDA) and Pulmonary Artery Stenosis.

Neurological & Late Manifestations

Neurological Impact

  • Microcephaly and intellectual disability.
  • Progressive Rubella Panencephalitis (PRP): Rare fatal late deterioration.

Autism Spectrum Behavior

Studies show a significantly higher prevalence of autism spectrum behaviors in children born with CRS.

Aims of the Study

1. Clinical Detection

Detect Rubella-specific antibodies (IgG/IgM) in pregnant women with history of complications.

2. Risk Evaluation

Correlate RuV infection with rates of recurrent miscarriages.

3. Public Awareness

Highlight the need for mandatory pre-marital screening and vaccine coverage.

Research Methodology

  • Design: Cross-sectional descriptive study.
  • Sample: 75 pregnant women with obstetric complications in Baghdad.
  • Assay: Rubella IgG/IgM Rapid Test Cassette.

Discussion

High Complication Burden

The 56.3% recurrence rate emphasizes the silent impact of TORCH infections, specifically Rubella, in Baghdad hospitals.

Age Risk Factors

Peak complication rates match prime reproductive years, making screening before pregnancy essential.

Discussion (Continued)

Socioeconomic Trends

Urban stress and pollution might contribute to increased risk profiles compared to simpler rural settings.

Fitness Benefits

Higher maternal physical fitness levels showed a clear trend toward improved fetal survival rates.

Discussion: Vitamin A Toxicity Theory

An intriguing metabolic observation in Rubella pathogenesis.

  • Rubella infection induces hepatic damage.
  • Liver cell lysis spills stored retinoids (Vitamin A) into circulation.
  • Causes temporary Functional Hypervitaminosis A.

Retinoid Spillage Consequences:

Increased intracranial pressure, accelerated bone resorption, and teratogenic organ malformations.

Recommendations

1. Compulsory Screening

Enforce strict mandatory pre-marital Rubella antibody testing in health protocols.

2. Targeted Immunization

Ensure wide vaccination campaigns targeting women of childbearing age.

Conclusion

Rubella remains a entirely preventable tragedy. Through screening protocols and comprehensive vaccination programs, Congenital Rubella Syndrome can be fully eradicated.
